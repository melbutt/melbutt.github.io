About

Hi there!

I am a PhD researcher at the University of British Columbia. I am studying Cognitive Psychology under the supervision of Dr. Todd C. Handy, working out of the Attentional Neuroscience Lab.

Currently, my research focuses on variability in daily cognition in young adults, particularly how subjective assessments of cognitive abilities are influenced by variation in mood, and how these effects differ across individuals. You can read more about my research below.

Research

Daily Cognitive Variability

My primary area of research examines intraindividual variability in daily cognition in young adults. Broadly, I am interested in challenging the assumption that cognitive abilities remain stable across situations and timescales in young, healthy populations. In particular, I focus on establishing cognitive variability as a meaningful measure beyond mean-level performance, while also advancing research on subjective cognition and promoting its recognition as an important indicator of wellbeing.

Community Mobility

My secondary area of research concerns community mobility, conceptualized as patterns of out-of-home movement. I am interested in how individuals interact with and navigate their communities in daily life. Using passive GPS tracking (e.g., distance travelled, locations visited, and time spent away from home), I aim to characterize daily mobility patterns and understand how they relate to cognitive health and wellbeing.

Transportation Planning

Stemming from my interest in community mobility and understanding human daily movement patterns, I have pursued collaborations with researchers at UBC's School of Community and Regional Planning. Together, we investigated the processes contributing to the green-gentrification cycle in Seattle, Washington. Using state sequence analysis, we clustered neighbourhoods based on the timing and sequencing of transit, bike infrastructure, and green space investments.
